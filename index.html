I AM Performance / Coaching

Team Performance

Every movement is measured against each coach’s own baseline variation, so real change is separated from ordinary month-to-month noise.

At a glance

01Signal board

Each coach is compared against their own trailing baseline, not the team average. A drop only counts as a signal once it clears the noise in that coach’s own history. Ordered by distance from normal — select any card to drive the charts below.

02This period

Rate per session is a weighted figure — total revenue divided by total sessions across the window, not an average of coach rates. The two differ whenever coaches carry different volumes.

How “team vs baseline” is worked out. Take the team’s total sessions for the window you have selected — one month, the average of the last three, or of the last twelve. The baseline is the average of every month that came before the window. The plain-English line shows the window as a straight percentage above or below that baseline, so “+8% vs its baseline of 720 sessions/mo” means the current window is running 8% busier than the team’s own historical norm. The σ figure beside it expresses the same gap in units of how much this team normally bounces around month to month — useful because a steady team and a wildly swinging one can both be “8% up”, but only for the steady team is that unusual. As a rough guide, under 2σ is ordinary variation, 2 to 3σ is worth a look, and beyond 3σ is genuinely unusual. The baseline deliberately excludes the window being judged, so a strong run cannot quietly raise the bar it is measured against. On All time there is no earlier period left to compare with, so the figure is not shown.

03Coach detail

The two column groups cover different spans and should not be read across as one row of like-for-like figures. Left group is the latest month only — sessions, revenue, rate and assessments as they actually landed that month. Right group is that coach’s whole history. Two z columns on purpose: z (latest) is how far the latest month sits from the full-history baseline — the classic single-month check — and stays put whatever the control is set to. z (window) tracks the dropdown and answers a different question: how far the mean of the selected window sits from the history before it. At 1m the two columns match, which is the fastest way to see the control is doing what it says. Beside z (latest) is P​NN, the empirical percentile — the share of the coach’s past months at or below this level, with the matching top X% shown next to it so there is no inverted reading. P90 means a high month: 90% of months were at or below it, so it ranks in the top 10%. P50 is a median month; P10 is a low point, in the bottom 10%. Only coaches working in the latest month appear here. A blank assessment cell counts as 0 rather than missing data. Conversion rate stays blank where there were no assessments to convert. Baseline is the trailing mean of every month before the latest, so it is a stable reference; CV is consistency, standard deviation as a share of the coach’s own average. Any column sorts.

04Target attainment

How each active coach’s actual output compares to a target. Manual lets a monthly session or revenue target be typed per coach — useful once the CEO or the coach has agreed one. Equal share assumes every active coach should carry the same load, so the target for each is the team total for the window divided across the current active headcount. Attainment is actual as a share of target; gap is the same figure in absolute units. A separate section from the coach detail on purpose — z tells you if a coach shifted against their own history, attainment tells you if they hit a number that was set for them.

05Trend and normal range

The moving average smooths ordinary variation; the bands show how wide “normal” actually is for this series. Points outside ±3σ are rare — roughly one month in 370 if nothing has genuinely changed. When the band width is set above 1σ, the inner σ levels are drawn as faint reference lines so you can see how far a point crossed, not just that it did. Click any item in the legend to show or hide it on the chart.

Drag across the chart to zoom into a span, scroll to zoom in and out, and hold Shift and drag to pan while zoomed. Reset zoom returns to the full range. Mark month drops a labelled reference line — add as many as you like (hire dates, price changes, quarter starts) and remove them from the chips below.

06Projection

A forward estimate built from each active coach’s own recent output, not a single team trendline — so it survives changes in team size and reflects who is actually on the floor. For each coach it takes their trailing average (the last 3, 6 or 12 months, or their full history), optionally scales it by the seasonality index for the target month, and where you have entered planned leave it removes the days that coach will be away. The team figure is the sum of the coaches. The shaded shells around the projection are ±1, ±2 and ±3 standard deviations, built from each coach’s own month-to-month volatility — darkest for the ±1σ core where the outcome most likely lands, fading out to ±3σ which almost always contains it. This is a planning aid, not a promise: it assumes each coach keeps converting their available days at their normal rate, and it cannot know about a coach quietly ramping down, a new signing about to ramp up, or a package mix shift. Treat the leave-adjusted number as the realistic floor and the raw number as the ceiling.

On the base window. A short window (3 months) tracks recent form and reacts fast; a long one or full history is steadier and smooths out one-off spikes. Full history pulls in a coach’s entire record, so for a coach whose output has genuinely shifted it can lag reality — recent windows suit a changing team, longer windows suit a stable one. The chart shows the full actual record back to August 2022 by default; use Chart from to scale into any recent span, and Log scale to keep the early months legible — team output stepped roughly twentyfold when the second gym was taken over in mid-2024, which flattens everything before it on a linear axis. Actual points that were once predicted are coloured on the chart itself — green sat inside ±1σ, amber ±2σ, orange ±3σ, red beyond it — with the full breakdown in the track record below.

Typical for this period — average and median

Plain descriptive numbers for whatever window Base on above is set to — last 9 months, since Jun 2024, last 3 years, however it's set. Median matters alongside average because one unusually strong or weak month can pull an average a fair way from what a typical month actually looks like; when the two are close, the period has been fairly steady, when they diverge, a few outlier months are doing the pulling. Rate per session is each month's own revenue over its own sessions, then averaged/medianed across the window — not one blended figure for the whole period.

Revenue per coach — across periods

Every ranked coach's own real monthly revenue, pooled together (not team revenue split evenly by headcount), across a fixed set of windows. This is the more reliable read of "typical revenue per coach": a much larger sample for the same window, it reflects what coaches actually earned, and it doesn't hide the spread between a strong month and a weak one the way an even split does. The team÷headcount column is shown alongside for reference, but treat the pooled average and median as the primary numbers.

Quarter / half-year / year projection

Same idea as the monthly projection above, at a coarser grain. If the current quarter, half-year or year is still filling in, the useful number is an estimate of how it will finish — actual-to-date plus a trailing run rate for the months still open — shown alongside the plain projection for the period after that. Uses the Scope, Measure, Base on and Range settings from above; a calendar anchor (since Jan <year>, since Jun 2024) filters which complete periods count, a trailing month-count has no equivalent at this grain so it falls back to using everything on record. The sample is naturally small here, which is fine for a directional read but means the range should be trusted less than the monthly one.

Track record — was the projection right?

Every month, the first time it comes within reach of a projection (up to 6 months out), gets locked in here — the prediction never changes afterwards, even as the base window or seasonality settings get tuned later. History is reconstructed the same way: each past month's prediction is rebuilt using only the data that would have existed at that point in time, never data from after it, so this is a genuine backtest and not hindsight. Once real data for a month lands, it is compared against exactly what was predicted. If the model is well calibrated, roughly 68% of months should land within ±1σ, and about 95% within ±2σ. Far fewer than that means the projection is more volatile than it lets on; far more means the band is wider than it needs to be.

Which base window actually works best?

Re-runs the same backtest under every base-window option in turn — last 3/6/12 months, since each January, and all history — for the scope and measure currently selected above, so the choice is based on how each setting actually would have performed rather than a guess. This does not touch or change the live track record above; it is a scratch test. Takes a few seconds since it repeats the full history multiple times.

07Year on year

Every calendar year drawn as its own line across Jan–Dec, so the same month can be read across years directly. This answers a different question from the seasonality index above: that one averages years together to find the underlying shape, this one keeps them apart so you can see whether the current year is running above or below where the last one was at the same point. Part-years simply stop where the data stops — 2022 begins in August because that is when records begin, and the current year runs to the latest close and extends itself as new months arrive. Click any year in the legend to show or hide it.

Why normalise. Headcount went from one coach through most of 2023 to three by May 2024, then jumped to seven in June 2024 when the second gym was taken over, and stands at nine now. On raw total almost all of the apparent growth is simply more people, which tells you the business got bigger but nothing about whether it got better. The divisor is read from the data month by month, so any future hire, departure or second takeover is absorbed automatically with nothing to update by hand. Select Coaches active as the measure to see the staffing curve itself.

Mean or median — this choice changes the answer. Mean per coach is the team total divided by the number of coaches who delivered that month. It is the true average, but it is pulled by whoever sits at the extremes: one coach on a light month drags it down for everybody. Median per coach is the middle coach — half the team above, half below — and it is barely affected by one unusual figure at either end. In this data the two genuinely diverge: in June 2026 the mean was 25,928 while the median was 32,402, a 20% gap, because a single low month sits well below the rest of the team. Read the median when the question is “what does a typical coach produce”, and the mean when the question is “what does the average headcount cost or return” — for budgeting, the mean is the right one precisely because it includes the weak months. Where the two are far apart, that gap is itself the finding: it means the team is uneven, not that one number is wrong.

Indexed — year start = 100. Each year is rebased so its own first month equals 100, and every later month is shown as a percentage of that start. Level differences vanish entirely and only the shape of each year’s run remains, so a thin early year and a full recent one can be compared on equal terms. This is the same idea as a rebased return chart: it answers “which year built momentum through the year, and how does the current one compare at the same point” rather than “which year was bigger”. It deliberately replaces the value axis rather than sitting on a second one — an index and an absolute amount are different units, and stacking them on one chart across five year-lines reads as clutter rather than insight.

08Seasonality

How to read the index. Each month is expressed as an index where 100 = an average month for whichever scope is selected. Below 100 = seasonally soft, above 100 = seasonally strong. So an index of 111 in June means June runs roughly 11% above a typical month; 80 in August means August runs 20% below.
Raw vs normalised. Team headcount grew from one coach in 2023 to nine in 2026. On raw team total, later months look seasonally strong when they were actually just staffed better — useful for absolute capacity planning, misleading as a seasonal read. On per coach (normalised), each coach’s months are divided by that coach’s own annual average before being averaged across the team, so the seasonal shape is separated from the hiring story. Normalised is the honest seasonal read; the raw view is only for absolute capacity comparisons.
Confidence. Driven by how many complete calendar years feed the scope. Good = 3+ full years, dependable pattern. Fair = 2 full years, plan-worthy but not certain. Weak = 1 full year, treat as a hypothesis to confirm rather than a forecast. None = no complete year, do not rely on it.

10Assessment conversion

Assessments held versus those that became paying clients — the strongest leading indicator available, moving weeks ahead of revenue.

11Assessment deep dive

Read the sample size before the percentage. Across the whole record there are fewer than a hundred assessments spread over more than a year, which works out to a handful a month across the whole team. At that volume a single outcome swings a monthly rate by tens of points, and several months showing “100%” rest on one or two assessments. Every figure here is therefore shown with its counts, and the shaded band is a 95% confidence interval — the range the true conversion rate plausibly sits in given how few observations there are. Where a band is wide, the month is telling you almost nothing. Where two bands overlap, the difference between them is not established. This is the difference between a number that looks precise and one that is.

Pooling adds the counts across neighbouring months before dividing, rather than averaging the percentages. That is the correct way to combine small samples — it weights each month by how many assessments it actually contained, and the confidence band narrows as real evidence accumulates.

12Concentration risk

How much of the month’s revenue rests on each coach. If one person carries too large a share, their notice period becomes a business risk rather than a staffing one.

Concentration score over time

The same score, every month since the earliest month with revenue data. Dashed lines mark the watch (18) and concentrated (25) thresholds. Updates automatically whenever a new month is imported or added.

13Group classes

Group-class participation, tracked entirely separately from personal training. Every figure here is attendance — how many people showed up — not revenue, and none of it feeds the PT rate-per-session. The point is to see which classes fill the room and which are fading, so strong formats can be pushed and weak ones replaced. Monthly totals reconcile to the source report. A class marked stopped simply had no session in the most recent month.

14Roster

Marking someone as departed keeps every month they worked in the trend and seasonality charts, but removes them from the current month — from the signal board, the coach table, concentration and the team totals. Nothing is deleted.

15Import a report

Drop in an xlsx, csv or pdf. Four formats are recognised automatically. The monthly matrix — your Session_rate working file, one row per coach with the months spread across the columns — is read directly: it pulls PT sessions and PT revenue for every coach and every month in the sheet, ignoring the group-class columns (those live in the Group classes section, never in the PT rate). A finance report (one tidy row per coach per month) is matched by heading. An assessment log, whether an xlsx export or the monthly PDF report, is rolled up per coach per month — only rows marked Done count, and only a positive converted marker counts as converted, so TBC, no-reply and scheduled are excluded. This tool tracks exactly one thing from an assessment report: held and converted, per coach, per month — nothing else in the report (client names, sessions availed) is read, to keep the parser tied to what actually gets tracked. A PDF report often carries more than one month at once as TBC rows get carried forward; only the single month due next after whatever is already on record gets imported, others are left out on purpose and picked up when their turn comes. Assessment months replace the prior reading; matrix and finance months fill sessions and revenue while leaving assessments untouched. Blank cells never overwrite existing values. Nothing is written until you confirm the preview.
